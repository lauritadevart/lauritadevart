# Abstract

This diploma thesis proposes the automation of subprocesses associated with line 320 of the 300 rolling mill at Antillana de Acero, using the Siemens S7-1500 series. The main objective is to modernize the currently obsolete automatic control system to improve efficiency and reduce production downtime. Line 320 operates with two GE Fanuc series 90-30 PLCs, which are outdated and face communication issues, making maintenance and operation difficult. The proposal includes migrating to Siemens S7-1500 PLCs and implementing two screens in a SCADA system to monitor and control the rolling process.

The thesis is structured into four chapters. In Chapter 1, the state of the art in rolling technology is described, both internationally and nationally, highlighting advancements in the steel industry, such as cold rolling and the integration of Industry 4.0 technologies. The rolling process at Antillana de Acero is also detailed, including the main components and instruments used. In Chapter 2, the automation proposal is presented, with the selection of the Siemens S7-1500 PLC and the design of flowcharts to control subprocesses in line 320, such as drags and shears. Chapter 3 addresses the monitoring interface and communication network, using Siemens WinCC V7.5 software for real-time supervision. Finally, in Chapter 4, a technical-economic analysis is conducted, evaluating the project's feasibility and its impact on production, the environment, and the company's economy.

The project concludes that modernizing the automatic control system will significantly improve the efficiency of the 300 rolling mill, reducing downtime and increasing product quality. Additionally, it highlights that the implementation will resolve current communication and maintenance issues, enabling Antillana de Acero to compete in more demanding markets.

Keywords: automation, rolling mill, PLC, Siemens S7-1500, SCADA, Antillana de Acero.
