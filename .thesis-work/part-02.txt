## Problem situation

The finishing line L320 of the 300 rolling mill has two obsolete, discontinued GE Fanuc series 90-30 PLCs whose manufacturer no longer exists. These are modular PLCs using the SNP-X communication protocol, with which communication problems exist. Replacing and repairing the modules of the existing PLCs is difficult because they are obsolete. Technology that is too simple is used for a process that should have more redundancy and sophistication. The control is unsatisfactory and does not serve the process efficiently, causing stoppages, delays in fault repair, and an increase in the frequency of faults.

## Problem to be solved

A migration to Siemens S7-1500 PLCs must be carried out as a modernization of the existing PLCs in the finishing and roughing lines of the 300 rolling mill. The implementation of a SCADA system is also considered in order to properly attend to the process.

## 1.1 Introduction

In the field of the metallurgical industry and materials processing, rolling mills play a fundamental role in the production of sheets, plates, and metal profiles. Automation not only ensures greater precision and repeatability in the process, but also optimizes resource use, reduces production times, and minimizes human errors. ## 1.2 State of the art

### 1.2.1 International panorama

Rolling is a key process in steel transformation and the manufacture of metallic products. It is based on the deformation of the metal by the compressive force exerted by two rollers rotating in opposite directions which, through friction, change the dimensions of the steel being processed. Historically, and generally, this process has been carried out exclusively hot, since the material being transformed requires great flexibility and low residual stress. However, innovation has recently appeared in cold rolling.

The cold rolling sector has established itself as a fundamental pillar within the global steel industry, enabling the creation of steel products of excellent quality that are indispensable in multiple sectors. From the automotive industry to construction and electronics, this process guarantees precision, durability, and impeccable finishes, making it a key element of contemporary manufacturing.

During cold rolling, steel is subjected to the action of rollers at temperatures below its recrystallization point. This method not only strengthens the metal, increasing its tensile strength, but also improves its granular structure. As a result, a material with a superior surface finish and more precise dimensional tolerances is obtained compared with hot-rolled steel. Among its advantages are a more uniform surface and constant thickness, which are essential in industries where aesthetics and accuracy are priorities. Likewise, this type of steel offers greater strength and longevity, making it ideal for applications that must withstand extreme stresses and wear conditions. In addition, the process allows thinner and lighter sheets to be manufactured, which are in high demand in sectors such as vehicle body manufacturing and lightweight structures.

The adoption of Industry 4.0 technologies, such as the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence, and big data analytics, is revolutionizing this market. These tools enable greater automation, more precise process control, and better-founded decisions. For example, IoT sensors can monitor critical variables in real time, such as temperature, pressure, and roller alignment, generating data that allow rolling to be optimized. For their part, artificial intelligence and machine-learning algorithms analyze this information to anticipate equipment failures, improve maintenance plans, and increase operational efficiency.
